# Version 1 — The torque report

**Date:** 2026-11-18
**Status:** implemented 2026-11-18 (short design, placeholder servo currents accepted). Result: all 48 rows pass; worst peak 1.26 N·m (43% of stall, wave sideways), worst RMS 0.43 N·m (44% of rated), no step at the actuator limit, margin never under 70 mm. See `docs/reports/v1-torque-report.md`.
**Size:** a small project
**Roadmap step:** 1.8, and the exit-test box "a torque report exists" in [03 — Roadmap](../../03-roadmap.md)

## Intent

The torque report is the evidence for the first purchase: the servo decision rule in the
roadmap needs peak and average joint torque per gait, from the simulated walker, set against
the candidate servo's numbers ([06 — Simulation](../../06-simulation.md), "The torque report";
[10 — Components](../../10-components.md)). It is generated by a script and saved with the version
tag, and again in version 1.5 with CAD masses.

## Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals:** a script that runs a fixed matrix of simulated walks and writes a Markdown report and
a JSON file; per-joint statistics measured on **every 2 ms physics step**; a verdict per row
against the decision rule.

**Non-goals:** real hardware numbers (version 2 replaces the placeholders); battery sizing
beyond a power estimate; the 1.5 servo decision itself.

**Anti-goals:** a report that cannot fail. The simulator clips torque at 1.47 N·m (50% of stall,
the design limit), so a report measured under that clip can never show a peak above it.

## Decisions

- **Measure demand, not the clipped value.** The report runs with the torque clip at the
  servo's stall torque (2.94 N·m) so peaks show what the walk asks for, compares the peak with
  the 50% rule (1.47 N·m) itself, and counts the physics steps that would have saturated the
  design limit. The walking tests keep the 1.47 N·m limit.
- **Matrix** (a call of mine): forward walking for each gait at 25%, 50% and 100% of its top
  speed; sideways and turning in place at each gait's top speed; standing; each at total robot
  masses 2.0, 2.2 and 2.5 kg (the chassis mass changes; the six 185 g legs stay). 48 runs (a stand, then per gait forward at three speeds, sideways and turn, at each mass).
- **Statistics:** per joint, from every physics step: peak |torque|, RMS torque, mean |torque|,
  peak joint speed; plus the count of steps at the limit. Peak is over the whole run including
  start-up; RMS, mean and power are over the steady part (after a 6 s warm-up), because they
  predict heating and battery use.
- **Rows also record** the minimum stability margin and the loaded foot slip (doc 06 lists both).
- **Verdicts:** peak torque of the worst joint under 50% of stall; worst RMS under the rated
  torque (0.98 N·m for the 12 V STS3215, doc 10); peak speed against the no-load 4.7 rad/s (the
  loaded speed is lower and unknown, so this is a ceiling, not a pass mark).
- **Electrical power estimate:** the brushed-motor model per servo,
  `P = V · (I_idle + |τ| / k_t)` with `k_t = stall torque / stall current`, summed over 18
  servos, with the mean |τ| of each joint. **Placeholder servo constants**, flagged in the
  parameter file and in doc 10 for confirmation against the exact sub-model: 12 V, stall
  current 2.7 A, idle current 0.1 A, rated torque 0.98 N·m (rated torque is from doc 10).
- **Output:** `docs/reports/v1-torque-report.md` and `.json`, committed so they travel with
  the `v1` tag. The JSON holds all 18 joints for every row; the Markdown shows the worst joint of
  each type (coxa, femur, tibia) per row and a full 18-joint table for the top-speed rows at 2.5 kg.

## Approaches considered

Measure under the design clip (simplest, but cannot fail: rejected); measure with the clip at
stall (chosen); measure with no clip at all (shows demand beyond what the servo could ever give,
which is not a pass-or-fail for a real servo: rejected).

## What is tested

The statistics and the saturation count against hand-computed values on a tiny scenario; the
power model against hand arithmetic; verdict logic at the boundaries; the report text for a
fabricated result set; one real scenario run for a few seconds. The full matrix is run by
the tool, not by the test suite.
